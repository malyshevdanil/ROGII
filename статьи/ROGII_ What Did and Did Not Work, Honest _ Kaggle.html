ROGII: What Did and Did Not Work, Honest

ROGII: What Did and Did Not Work, Honest

Tracking the structural surface from GR: an honest particle-filter pipeline and a map of what generalizes (and what doesn't).

Project Writeup · Jul 4, 2026

Reading the Structural Surface: an Honest Particle-Filter Pipeline for Wellbore TVT, and What Did and Did Not Transfer

Working Note — ROGII Wellbore Geology Prediction (Kaggle).
Author: Julian Camilo Villa Duque.

The problem in plain terms (a 60-second primer)

Geosteering means steering a horizontal well to stay inside a thin target rock layer while drilling.
The well drops vertically, curves to horizontal, and then runs along the layer for thousands of feet. The
rock's gamma-ray (GR) log is a fingerprint of which layer the bit is in; a nearby vertical
type-well gives the reference mapping GR → TVT (which GR pattern corresponds to which stratigraphic
depth).

What we predict. For each 1-ft step along the horizontal, the target is TVT — the bit's stratigraphic position (where it sits within the rock column). TVT is given only up to a
Prediction-Start (PS) point; past PS it is hidden and must be predicted from the GR trace and the
type-well. The score is the RMSE of the predicted TVT.

Figure 0 — The setup. A horizontal well is steered inside a target layer (orange). Its map position, its true vertical depth Z, and its GR are known everywhere; its stratigraphic position TVT is known only up to the PS point and must be predicted afterward (red zone), by matching the GR trace against the vertical type-well.

What is measured at each point (and what is known at test time):

Variable What it is Known at test?
MD distance drilled along the well (1-ft steps) yes, always
X, Y map position (plan view) yes, always
Z true vertical depth (TVD) yes — the whole lateral
GR gamma-ray (rock radioactivity) — the layer "fingerprint" yes (~29% missing)
TVT the bit's stratigraphic position = the target only up to PS; hidden after
type-well vertical reference: GR(TVT) + geology yes, always
ANCC…BUDA formation-top depths (the structural surface) train only

The one identity that makes it tractable: TVT = S − Z, where S = TVT + Z is the smooth
structural surface (it changes slowly as the layer dips). Since Z is known along the whole lateral,
predicting the smooth S and subtracting the known Z is far easier than predicting the oscillating
TVT directly — the backbone of everything below.

Abstract

I predict True Vertical Thickness (TVT) along the un-logged tail of a horizontal well from the
gamma-ray (GR) log. My final pipeline is a sequential particle filter (PF) that tracks the smooth
structural surface S = TVT + Z and a local dip, weighted by GR likelihood against the type-well,
augmented by a confidence-gated spatial prior, a physics post-process, a gradient-boosted meta-layer,
and a decorrelated beam tracker (public LB 7.72). This note is deliberately weighted toward
what I learned and what failed, because on this task the negative results are where the value is.
My headline conclusions: (1) the target reduces to a per-well offset plus a piecewise dip read off the
type-well, and the six "formation" columns are a single surface with constant offsets, not six
degrees of freedom; (2) the hard part is a bimodal stratigraphic datum on a minority of wells that
is largely irreducible; (3) the disciplined validation lesson — only distribution-shift hold-outs are trustworthy, and run-to-run PF seed variance (~0.1 ft) swamps most "improvements" — is what kept me
honest; and (4) a broad sweep of plausible additions (learned residual correctors, DTW/NCC matchers, a
second "decorrelated" PF, richer formation features, more training data, and a bimodal heel-calibration
hedge) did not transfer, for reasons that line up with the community's own findings. I report the
per-component contribution of everything that did work, and I am candid that my result sits in the
public fork cluster rather than at the board heads.

A note on scope: I share the reasoning, the data insights, and every negative result in full — that is where the community value lies. I describe the architecture and each component's quantified contribution; exact features and constants are omitted while the competition is live and will be provided to the Sponsor with the reproducible solution as required.

1. The task, and the physical identity it reduces to (Criterion 3)

Each well is a horizontal trajectory with MD, X, Y, Z, GR, a known TVT_input up to a
prediction-start point, and a vertical type-well (GR vs TVT). I predict TVT on the hidden tail, where
X/Y/Z are known. The metric is pooled row-wise RMSE.

Two physical facts shaped everything:

  • S = TVT + Z is a smooth structural surface. Since Z is known on the tail, predicting the smooth
    S and recovering TVT = S − Z is far better-posed than predicting the oscillating TVT directly.
    The task becomes tracking a slowly-varying surface plus a dip.
  • TVT = surface − Z + b_well, with b_well constant per well (residual std ≈ 0.006 ft). The
    offset is pinned by the heel; the surface and its dip are what must be read. I build every component
    in these terms, which keeps the solution a physically-meaningful interpretation rather than a
    metric-optimizer.

Figure 1 — The structural surface S = TVT + Z is smooth and the well's Z (TVD) is known everywhere, so predicting S and subtracting the known Z is far better-posed than predicting the oscillating TVT directly. Every component of my pipeline is expressed in terms of this surface and its dip.

I treat these as community-established (franticXu, Tabish, pilkwang, souldrive) and take no credit for
them; my contribution is the pipeline and the map of what is and is not recoverable.

2. Insights about the data and the wells (Criterion 2)

2.1 The six "formation" columns are one surface, not six. ANCC, ASTNU, ASTNL, EGFDU, EGFDL, BUDA
collapse to one base surface plus five constant per-well offsets (layer-thickness std < 0.01 ft) — one
degree of freedom, derived from the type-well's TVT boundaries, not measured 3D surfaces. This is why
(2.5) my attempt to use all six as independent structural priors added nothing over one.

2.2 The columns are absent at test. Train wells carry the six surfaces and TVT; test wells ship with only MD, X, Y, Z, GR, TVT_input. Any structural prior must therefore be imputed from offset wells, not read from the well's own columns — a subtlety that silently invalidates otherwise-attractive
features (I learned this the hard way, §5).

2.3 The recoverable/irreducible split. A calibrated oracle ladder (fit the simplest shape to the
true tail) reads roughly: carry-last ~16, best per-well offset ~9, best per-well line ~6.6, robust
smooth fit ~3.0. The public fork cluster (~7.2) has captured offset + dominant slope; the recoverable
floor for the median well is ~3–5 ft. So the gap below the cluster is a matching-quality problem on
the median wells, not a wall. (Ladder and framing after Georgy Mamarin's public diagnostic.)

*Figure 2 — The recoverable / irreducible map. Bars are train oracle ceilings (each fits the
simplest shape to the *true* tail, so it is unreachable for that shape). Dashed lines are honest
public-LB scores. The fork cluster (~7.2) has captured offset + dominant slope; the smooth oracle at
~3.0 says real structure remains below even the board heads — the gap is matching quality on the
median wells, not a wall. My pipeline (7.72) sits in the cluster.*

2.4 The hard part is a bimodal datum. The Eagle Ford is rhythmically bedded (Milankovitch couplets,
~15–25 ft), so the horizontal GR lines up with the type-well at two stratigraphic positions ~one bundle apart (±~15 ft), and on a truly ambiguous well it is close to a coin-flip which is real. This
minority (~10% of wells) carries ~40% of the squared error. The decision-theoretic consequence
(pilkwang): on such a well the squared-loss-optimal estimate is the posterior mean p·a + (1−p)·b,
not a committed mode; committing to a mode is a trap that raises expected error (§5, §4-below).

2.5 The GR match is confounded with absolute position. The GR fingerprints lithology, but GR(TVT)
repeats, so the best global GR match points confidently at a wrong depth. An oracle registration
that sees the true eval GR and grid-searches offset+slope scores ~31 ft (worse than carrying flat),
recovering the slope at ~zero correlation. What GR carries is relative and coarse, best read
sequentially with the offset pinned — which is exactly what a particle filter does, and why unbounded
matchers "lie confidently." This is the single most important thing I internalized, and it explains
several of my negative results below.

Figure 3 — Why the best GR fit can be the wrong depth. The Eagle Ford's rhythmic bedding makes GR(TVT) repeat (left), so the observed GR window matches the type-well at two stratigraphic positions ~one bundle apart (~±15 ft). The GR-misfit as a function of a datum shift (right) then has two comparable minima — an irreducible coin-flip on the ~10% of wells that carry ~40% of the error. The squared-loss-optimal response is the posterior mean p·a+(1−p)·b, not a committed mode.

3. Method (Criterion 3 — every stage has a physical reading)

The pipeline is a physical tracker with a bounded meta-layer on top — the learned part corrects, it
does not replace:

  per-well GR + type-well ─▶ ┌──────────────────┐
                             │  PARTICLE FILTER │  track S = TVT+Z + dip,
                             │   (multi-seed)   │  GR-likelihood, BOUNDED search
                             └────────┬─────────┘
   offset wells ─▶ gated structural prior ─┐  (blend only where neighbours dense)
                                           ▼
                             ┌────────────────────────┐
                             │  PHYSICS POST-PROCESS  │  robust projection of S
                             │  (project + damp + smooth) │  + warm-up + smoothing   ═▶  B
                             └───────────┬────────────┘
   beam tracker ┐                        │
   PF @ scales  ┼─▶ GBDT META-LAYER ─────┤  (heavily regularized, predicts TVT-delta)
   geometry/GR  ┘   (bounded correction) │
                             ▼            ▼
                    final  =  B  ⊕  GBDT   (blended toward the physics model B)
  1. Particle filter on S = TVT + Z + dip rate. State = (structural position, dip). Particles
    propagate along MD with a near-constant-dip model and small process noise, reweighted by the GR
    likelihood against the type-well; systematic resampling; a multi-seed likelihood-weighted ensemble.
    The search is bounded so it cannot chase a far-away decoy (§2.5).
  2. Confidence-gated structural prior. A neighbour surface imputed from offset wells is blended with
    the PF only where offset neighbours are dense (a distance gate) and clipped to a physical band —
    pure PF where the spatial evidence is weak.
  3. Physics post-process. A robust (IRLS/Tukey) low-degree projection of the structural surface
    tames wrong-branch excursions; a warm-up damping term suppresses spurious drift right after the
    anchor; light smoothing removes resampling jitter. All are distribution-independent regularizers.
  4. Gradient-boosted meta-layer. A heavily-regularized LightGBM+CatBoost on the TVT-delta target
    combines the PF (at several likelihood temperatures), the spatial prior, the beam tracker, and
    geometry/GR descriptors — blended toward the physics model, not replacing it.
  5. Beam-search tracker. A deterministic top-K dip-rate search, decorrelated from the Monte-Carlo PF;
    useful as a feature precisely because its errors are partially independent.

I deliberately included a component only if it had a physical reading and improved a distribution-shift
evaluation — not merely the leaderboard.

4. Breadth and depth of exploration (Criterion 1 — negatives valued equally)

Each entry is a genuinely distinct approach with its motivation, how I validated it, and the lesson.
Minor tuning is not counted as an approach.

What worked (transferred to the public test):

  • PF on S = TVT + Z. The right state parameterization mattered more than any downstream model:
    a large, consistent gain over a geometric baseline and over a neural regressor.
  • Confidence-gated spatial prior. Gating a spatial prior by local reliability transferred where a
    uniform blend hurt — lesson: never blend a spatial prior ungated.
  • Physics post-process (robust projection + damping + smoothing). My single largest honest gain;
    distribution-independent physics regularizers transfer.
  • GBDT meta over tracker features. A learned model helps only as a bounded meta-layer over physical predictors, not as a standalone regressor.
  • Beam tracker as a feature. Independence from the PF — a different search over the same cue — is
    what made a second tracker earn its keep.

What did NOT transfer (with analysis — and these corroborate the community consensus):

  • Neural regressor (1D U-Net + attention to the reference GR). Under-predicted drift and generalized
    poorly. Consistent with the board finding that self-trained heavy-ML tends to land below the fork
    wave (a Grandmaster's CNN/MTP stack reportedly sat mid-pack).

  • Pattern-match trackers (NCC, DTW) as predictors or anchored features. Individually catastrophic
    (no absolute anchor); as features they helped inconsistently because they are correlated with the PF
    (same GR-level cue). Lesson: a new tracker must use a different cue, not a re-alignment of the same
    one. (The board's aligner tournaments reach the same verdict — DTW invents movement; only a bounded,
    windowed shape-matcher earns a small, gated delta.)

  • A second, "decorrelated" particle filter (including a fuzzy-logic likelihood). A fuzzy/robust
    likelihood was as accurate as the Gaussian but ~0.96-correlated with it, so blending gained nothing;
    a pure-shape firing decorrelated (~0.3) but had no absolute anchor and diverged. This is a single-cue problem — only GR-level-vs-reference anchors absolute TVT, so any good PF is correlated
    with the base PF. (The board's own diagnosis: the two strongest public engines correlate ρ≈0.89, so
    re-blending them is flat — more signal needs a genuinely decorrelated source.)

  • Richer multi-formation features. The well's own six surfaces give near-exact TVT locally — but the
    test files lack those columns (§2.2), so this was a train-only artifact; the honest offset-imputed
    version added nothing (the surfaces are one DOF, §2.1). Lesson: verify a feature exists in the test data before trusting a local gain.

  • More training data / more particles / larger seed ensembles. Each improved local metrics and lost
    on the public test — the classic distribution-shift signature; with a shift, more capacity fits the
    wrong distribution.

  • Re-tuning the tracker's own constants (initial spread, process noise). Won under distribution-shift
    CV yet lost on the public test: the tracker constants are effectively calibrated to the test
    distribution, and re-fitting them on train overfits it.

  • Heel-calibrated bimodal hedge (the community's headline lever). I heel-calibrated the GR gain/
    offset (legal) and hedged the datum toward the two-minima posterior mean. On my pipeline it hurt
    (worse under every split, even restricted to genuinely bimodal wells). The reason is §2.5: the legal
    shift-scan is confounded and points at the wrong depth, so moving the prediction toward its minima
    degrades it. The honest reading — which matches the source diagnostic — is that GR alignment's value is
    as an uncertainty gate, not a TVT predictor; my PF's particle spread already carries that signal.

  • Better sequential matchers on the PF likelihood. Chasing the board heads (~5), I tried three
    physically-motivated matcher upgrades inside the PF: a GR-rotation denoise (rolling median) before
    matching, a heel-calibrated gain/offset in the point likelihood, and a windowed shape-correlation
    likelihood (match the local GR pattern, not the level). On a hard well sample none beat the plain
    level-anchored PF: denoise was mixed (better median, worse tail), heel-calibration hurt (the same
    confound as the hedge), and the shape term either diverged (no absolute anchor) or corrupted
    the good level anchor when multiplied in. This is consistent with the board's own aligner tournaments,
    where only a carefully-bounded windowed matcher earns a ~0.2 ft gated delta; a crude shape likelihood
    does not. The lesson: the level-anchored PF is near its frontier for these ideas, and the heads' edge
    is a genuinely better sequential matcher I did not reproduce.

The recurring meta-lesson: add physically-motivated, decorrelated components; validate on distribution-shift hold-outs; do not re-tune the tracker to the training distribution; and treat GR alignment as a confidence signal, not a mover.

5. Contribution of individual ideas (Criterion 4)

Honest public-test progression, one component per step:

Step Component added Public RMSE Δ
0 Particle filter (multi-seed ensemble) 8.72 —
1 + confidence-gated structural prior 8.63 −0.09
2 + physics post-process (robust projection + damping + smoothing) 8.06 −0.57
3 + GBDT meta over tracker features 7.81 −0.25
4 + decorrelated beam tracker 7.72 −0.09

Figure 4 — Each component's contribution on the public LB. The physics post-process (Step 2) is the largest honest gain; the meta-layer and second tracker add real, independently-verified signal. The shaded band is the PF seed variance (~0.1 ft): the ±0.09 steps live inside it, so I read them as directional and seed-checked, not precise. My 7.72 sits inside the public fork cluster.

Two caveats I hold myself to. First, the ±0.09 steps are near the PF seed band — re-running the
same code reseeds the particle filter and moves the public score by a comparable amount (byte-identical
public notebooks span ~7.17–7.29), so I read those small steps as directional, seed-checked rather
than precise. Second, my 7.72 sits inside the public fork cluster, above the board heads (~5.3):
the honest gap to the front is better per-well GR matching, which I did not crack.

6. Uncertainty estimation (Criterion 5)

  • Particle spread (dispersion of the weighted cloud) is a per-point predictive variance; it widens
    on ambiguous GR and after long unconstrained intervals — exactly where the method is least reliable.
    Consistent with the community finding that GR-alignment spread correlates with error (~+0.2), I use
    it as a trust gate (a feature to the meta-layer), not as a predictor.
  • Neighbour distance gates the spatial prior: trusted where offset wells are dense, reverting to the
    pure PF where sparse — an explicit, physical confidence signal.
  • Cross-tracker agreement (spread across PF temperatures, the beam tracker, the spatial prior) lets
    the meta-layer down-weight regions where the trackers disagree.
  • Where I am weak, and know it: the ~10% bimodal wells (irreducible variance p(1−p)d²), and
    spatially sparse regions. I do not claim calibrated intervals; I claim ordered confidence, which
    is what I act on.

7. Reflection: genuine relationships vs optimizing the metric (Criterion 3)

My discipline was simple and, in hindsight, load-bearing: include a component only if it has a physical
reading and improves a distribution-shift evaluation, and treat any single-well or in-sample "gain" as
suspect until it clears the seed band and a shift split. That discipline is what let me reject my own
attractive-but-non-transferring ideas (the fuzzy second PF, the richer formation features, the
hyper-parameter re-tunes, the bimodal hedge) instead of shipping them. I think the most useful thing I
can offer the community is not my (average) score but this map of what generalizes and what does not on this task — and the confirmation, from an independent pipeline, of the recoverable/irreducible
structure and the single-cue limit.

8. Conclusion

The honest core for this task is a physically-grounded particle filter on S = TVT + Z, augmented
by a reliability-gated spatial prior, a physics post-process, a bounded gradient-boosted meta-layer, and
a decorrelated second tracker (public 7.72). The deeper contribution is methodological: the target
reduces to offset-plus-piecewise-dip; the six formation columns are one surface; the hard tail is a
bimodal datum best handled by a posterior mean, not a committed mode; GR matching is confounded with
absolute depth and must be bounded and used as a confidence signal; and — most practically —
distribution-shift validation plus a seed-variance check is the only trustworthy way to tell a real gain
from noise. I report, in equal measure, the components that transferred and the well-motivated ones that
did not, because on this problem the negative results carry as much information as the positive recipe.
