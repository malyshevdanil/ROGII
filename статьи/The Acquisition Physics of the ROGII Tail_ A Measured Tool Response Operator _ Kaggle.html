The Acquisition Physics of the ROGII Tail: A Measured Tool Response Operator

The Acquisition Physics of the ROGII Tail: A Measured Tool Response Operator

A measured tool response operator and coherence spectrum explain the ROGII alias tail and what does and does not close it.

Project Writeup · Jul 3, 2026

The Acquisition Physics of the ROGII Tail: A Measured Tool Response Operator that Bounds the Irreducible Core

ROGII Wellbore Geology Prediction. Working Note. (Submission-ready draft — corrections of 2026-07-01 applied; see WORKING_NOTE_CORRECTIONS.md for the audit trail.)

Abstract

We study why a minority of wells carry most of the error in this competition, and we contribute a measurement that,
to our knowledge, no public analysis has reported: the response operator that relates the wireline typewell gamma ray
to the logging while drilling gamma ray, recovered directly from the data, together with its coherence spectrum —
including a section-resolved version that shows the spectrum is markedly worse on the toe, the only rows the metric
scores. The community has already established the structure of the problem with unusual clarity. The tail is a bimodal
datum near plus or minus fifteen feet, driven by Milankovitch limestone and marl cyclicity, and on genuinely ambiguous
wells the posterior mean is the optimal prediction rather than either mode. The datum is largely recoverable by
calibrating the gamma ray gain and offset on each well's own known heel, which leaves only a small self similar core
that is truly ambiguous, and the remaining difficulty is the per well shape rather than the datum. Our measurement
explains this picture from the side of the instrument. Over the full lateral the correlation between the logging while
drilling curve and the typewell at the true depth is 0.755; on the heel it is 0.81; on the toe, where every scored row
lives, it falls to 0.665. The squared coherence tells the sharper story: it falls from about 0.62 at 125-foot
wavelengths to about 0.12 near five feet over the full lateral — and on the toe alone the entire five to thirty-two
foot band sits at 0.03 to 0.11, which removes exactly the fine cyclicity that would break the self similar tie. This
provides the acquisition physics behind the degenerate likelihood that others measured from the correlation side, and
it gives a quantitative reason why the small core resists even a perfect calibration. Around this measurement we
describe a deployable classical system that scores 6.955 on the public board, an exhaustive study of more than thirty
approaches — including three new negative results measured for this note that close the remaining classical escape
routes — an explicit account of where the method is uncertain, and the reproducible steps behind each result.

1. The task and what the community established

The per row target reduces to one number per well. With the structural surface defined as the sum of true vertical
thickness and the vertical coordinate, the competition identity makes the entire error a structural surface estimation
error, the six formation surfaces are parallel, and the surface is about ninety nine percent linear, so the problem is
to estimate a per well drift and a datum, with the intercept fixed by the known heel. We build on a body of public
analysis rather than restate it as our own. Several participants, notably the discussions by souldrive on the
Milankovitch cyclicity and by Georgy Mamarin on where the error lives, converged on a consistent picture, and we take
it as the starting point.

First, the tail is a bimodal datum. The Eagle Ford is a rhythmically bedded limestone and marl system whose couplets
repeat on a roughly fifteen to twenty five foot scale, so over a long lateral the horizontal gamma ray can align with
the typewell at two stratigraphic positions about one bundle apart, and both matches look about equally good. Second,
on a genuinely fifty fifty well the root mean squared error optimal prediction is the midpoint, near fifteen feet flat,
rather than either mode, which would average about twenty one feet, so a hedged prediction is mathematically preferred.
Third, the datum is largely legal to recover: fitting the gamma ray gain and offset on each well's own known heel
localizes the datum in about eighty percent of wells, close to an illegal oracle near eighty two percent, which leaves
a self similar core of roughly twelve percent as the genuine residue. Fourth, the harder open problem is the per well
shape, since the fine drift slope is not learnable from simple features on held out fields. Fifth, the strongest
reported result, about five feet of pooled row error from a single model that uses only a well and its own typewell,
shows that the shape is recoverable per well without any spatial information, so the frontier is a better per well
shape estimator rather than a neighbour model. We confirmed each of these from our own experiments, and we cite the
community for the framing.

A useful quantitative frame for what the leaders achieve: with the heel pinned, a perfect straight-line surface per
well floors the pooled error at 7.59 feet, and a perfect quadratic at 4.28 feet (all 773 training wells, 3.78 million
toe rows). The current leading scores sit strictly between these two oracles, so the leading edge necessarily models
per well curvature, not merely a better dip. The pooled metric's lever arm — the root mean square of measured depth
beyond the heel — is 3,124 feet, so a pooled score of 5.35 corresponds to a residual dip accuracy near one foot per
thousand feet on top of that curvature, about three percent of the natural well-to-well dip variation of 34 feet per
thousand feet.

2. A measured tool response operator, and its coherence spectrum

Our specific contribution begins from an observation about the known build section. Within the build, the logging while
drilling gamma ray and the wireline typewell gamma ray are both available at the same known depth, which gives ground
truth pairs from which the acquisition operator can be recovered. Across the training wells the operator is well
described by an affine term with gain near 0.79 and offset near 17.8 gamma ray units, plus a structured residual with
standard deviation near eleven units and a lag one autocorrelation near 0.83. The residual is therefore red rather than
white, and the depth lag is zero. The raw logging while drilling gamma ray has a standard deviation near 17 units. The
correlation between the logging while drilling curve and the typewell at the true depth is section dependent, and
stating it carefully matters: 0.755 over the full lateral, 0.81 on the heel where the calibration is fit, and 0.665 on
the toe where every scored row lives. The per well version of the affine fit is exactly the heel calibration that
others found to recover the datum, so our measurement characterizes the full operator that the winning calibration
exploits, including the parts a simple gain and offset do not capture — and it quantifies how much of the calibration's
apparent quality does not survive the trip from heel to toe.

The frequency content is the part that is genuinely new. Estimating the magnitude squared coherence between the two
curves as a function of gamma ray wavelength (Welch estimate, one-foot sampling) gives, over the full lateral, 0.62 at
125 feet, 0.45 at 60 feet, 0.38 at 32 feet, 0.24 at 14 feet, 0.15 at 8 feet, and 0.12 at 5 feet. The tool is a strong
low pass filter. Resolving the spectrum by section sharpens the conclusion considerably: on the heel the 14-foot
coherence is near 0.37 to 0.40, but on the toe the entire five to thirty-two foot band collapses to 0.03 to 0.11. Long
wavelength structure survives everywhere, but the fine five to fifteen foot cyclicity, which is exactly the
Milankovitch band that separates one alias branch from its neighbour, is effectively absent from the recorded signal on
the rows the competition scores. This is the mechanism behind the degenerate likelihood that others measured as a flat
gamma ray misfit at the true depth. The geology makes adjacent bundles self similar, and the instrument then removes
the fine detail that might still have distinguished them, so on the self similar core the information required to break
the tie is not concealed. It is largely absent from the recorded signal at usable fidelity. The coherence curve
therefore does more than restate the ambiguity. It quantifies why a perfect heel calibration still cannot resolve the
small core, and it predicts that no matched filter on the released gamma ray can, because the discriminating band is
below the instrument's information floor precisely where predictions are evaluated. One methodological caution we pass
on from our own audit: an earlier internal version of this analysis reported the toe band as "intact" at 0.36 to 0.47;
that figure was the square root of the coherence rather than the coherence, and we flag it so others do not repeat the
definitional slip.

3. The deployable system and the contribution of each idea

Our submitted system is an ensemble of gamma ray trackers followed by a learned combiner. The trackers are a family of
particle filters with several hundred particles and about a dozen dynamics and likelihood settings, a beam search over
the typewell grid, a multiscale normalized cross correlation against the typewell, and an ordinary kriging structural
prior from the formation point cloud. Their outputs feed three histogram gradient boosting models and two gradient
boosted tree models, reconciled by a non negative ridge regression and smoothed by a degree four post processing stage.
The table quantifies how the leading ideas contributed, using leave one well out cross validation for the classical
line and synthetic to real transfer for the neural line, which use different but internally consistent harnesses.

Two lessons are quantified here. The neighbour feature improved cross validation but harmed the public score; the
community has proposed a survey density explanation, in which neighbour error scales with dip rate times inter well
spacing, and we return to what we can and cannot currently verify about that explanation below. The measured operator
produced the largest single improvement in synthetic transfer, which is the practical evidence that the acquisition
physics of Section 2 matters. On the spatial question we add one clean number: measured on the tercile of training
wells whose nearest neighbour is beyond 632 feet, our per well stack degrades from 6.89 (dense two-thirds) to 10.41
pooled, while its median well stays near 5.6, so the distance penalty on this proxy is concentrated in the same alias
tail the rest of this note is about; we discuss the limits of this proxy immediately below.

4. An exhaustive search, and its convergent conclusion

We tested more than thirty genuinely different approaches, and a single structure recurred that matches the community
consensus: the answer is representable and not selectable. The most informative approaches follow, each with its idea,
validation, and lesson.

Diverse particle filtering reaches a median near 4.74 while the pooled tail stays near eight to ten, so it is excellent
on typical wells and capped by the branch tail. Structural kriging and analog surfaces improve cross validation from
8.02 to 7.68, yet regressed on the public board. We had attributed this to a short range structural correlation that
decays with distance and does not survive to a dispersed hidden field, and we want to be explicit about the state of
that claim rather than repeat it as settled. In a final audit for this note we could not recover the script, artifact,
or log behind the specific decay curve we had been citing, so we withdraw it as a precise number, and we found the
same gap behind a companion regional marker probe. Separately, and more consequentially, the belief that the hidden
test wells sit far from the training wells was itself a generalisation from three duplicate sample wells that we had
never independently checked; a legal score decoding submission described in the following paragraph shows the real
median distance from a hidden test well to its nearest training well is on the order of a thousand feet, not the
sixteen thousand feet we had assumed. This reopens rather than closes the neighbour and analog question, and we regard
the cause of the public regression as a genuinely open methodological question at the time of writing, one we are
actively re-examining, rather than a settled distance argument. We report this correction in the spirit of Section 5,
since a wrong but confident structural prior is exactly the failure mode our uncertainty framework is meant to catch,
and because a working note is a better place for a caught error than a silent one. Two dimensional signed
distance networks, following the published method, reach a pooled fifteen at the scales we could afford, and they track
the prior rather than surpass it. Synthetic pre training through the measured operator improves transfer from 19.97 to
15.93 but does not by itself close the gap to the classical stack, which is consistent with the leading edge being
synthetic scale on the order of a thousand times ours. A mixture output keeps several modes alive and a best of modes
readout reaches 5.6, while any committed readout lands near 9.3 to 15, which is the representable and not selectable
wall. A cycle spacing dip has a within well correlation of minus 0.19, because the tool has already removed that band.
A build section boundary condition gives a heel gradient dip correlation of 0.14 against 0.857 for simple geometric
extrapolation, so the heel dip is already well determined and the difficulty is drift further along the lateral.
Selection and routing reach an area under the curve of 0.673, a target oracle router only 7.48, and the best observable
gate 7.80, so selection is information bound. Posterior mean hedging matches the community result: on the ambiguous
core the midpoint is the Bayes hedge, worth about half a foot on the worst decile, while committing wins overall.
Finally, we examined the identifier and annotation leaks and confirmed they are inert on the hidden scored wells, so we
did not build on them.

Three further negative results were measured specifically for this note, because each is the natural "but surely"
objection to the coherence argument, and each fails in the way the measurement predicts. First, a joint two parameter
search — dip and datum together, scored by whole toe normalized cross correlation on a grid that provably contains the
truth — decodes to 81 feet pooled against a flat baseline of 15.4, with the correct alias branch chosen only 31 percent
of the time and a peak height that carries no confidence information; the family's own oracle on the same grid is 6.4
feet, so the failure is entirely in the objective, not the representation. Second, all of the linear family's
decodable slope turns out to be trajectory geometry: a heel pinned slope fit to the vertical coordinate alone, with the
gamma ray unused, decodes to 15.1 feet — indistinguishable from flat — and adding whole lateral gamma ray matching on
top does not improve it. Third, the smooth per well residual of our deployed stack is large and real — correcting each
well with its own oracle quadratic would take the pooled error from 8.20 to 3.29 — but those two coefficients per well
are not a function of anything computable from the well itself at test time: regressing them from geometry, long
wavelength gamma ray content, alias tie margins, and tracker disagreement yields negative out of fold R squared for
every model family we tried, and applying the shrunken corrections makes every cohort worse. The correction exists; the
information to select it does not travel with the well.

A fourth measurement closes the most tempting remaining objection — that wells drilled on the same pad could rescue
each other. We probed the hidden test set's spatial layout legally, through the public score of a deliberately offset
baseline submission, and found the test wells do cluster: the median distance from a test well to its nearest test
neighbour is one to three thousand feet, and half to three quarters have a neighbour within two thousand feet. The
channel this opens is same tool: two logging while drilling curves share the acquisition operator, and indeed their
mutual correlation in the true stratigraphic frame is 0.764, above the 0.665 of the cross tool channel. Yet every
mechanism built on it fails at the oracle level: consensus dip sharing fails because the true dip field decorrelates
between pad mates faster than a single well's own dip error (0.0023 versus 0.0016 feet per foot), and direct
log-to-log matching recovers the relative datum between pad mates to within three feet only twenty percent of the
time — with errors concentrated at multiples of seven feet. The well-to-well match aliases exactly as the
well-to-typewell match does. This is the sharpest form of the note's thesis: the ambiguity is the geology's
self-similarity, which the instrument compounds but did not create, and it survives every pairing of logs available
in this dataset.

The measured operator unifies these outcomes. A neighbour prior decays with distance and does not transfer. A selector
built on the fine cyclicity operates below the coherence floor. The recurring failure to select is the expected
consequence of a measured physical limit rather than a modelling shortfall.

5. Uncertainty estimation

The errors are concentrated and identifiable, not uniform, and the method estimates its own confidence in three ways.
Each particle filter reports the spread of its particle cloud, which broadens on ambiguous wells; the trackers disagree
on the same wells; and the kriging prior carries a variance that flags weak neighbour support. Together these give a
usable reliability signal, so predictions are trustworthy where the trackers agree and the cloud is tight, which is the
majority of wells and where the median sits near 4.8 feet, and unreliable on the roughly twelve percent self similar
core, where the gamma ray misfit at the candidate depth and one bundle away is a near tie. The honest and useful point
is that this core is detectable even though it is not correctable, because the coherence measurement predicts the
spacing of the second mode: probing the gamma ray misfit at plus or minus one bundle exposes a near tie rather than a
single minimum. On flagged wells the correct action is therefore to hedge toward the posterior mean rather than commit,
which spends down the irreducible squared error without pretending to recover a datum that the instrument did not
record.

6. Physical plausibility versus the metric

We drew the line between genuine physics and metric optimization deliberately. The central result, the measured tool
operator and its coherence spectrum, is a property of the acquisition rather than of the scoreboard, and the deployed
tracker estimates a dip and a surface consistent with the trajectory and the typewell. We identified an exact
identifier overlay that would have raised the public score, verified that it is inert on the hidden wells, and chose
not to depend on it because it encodes no physics and would not generalize. Where we accepted metric driven components,
such as the post processing smoother, we constrained them to be physically plausible, favouring smooth, heel anchored
surfaces over unconstrained fits. We also treated the public leaderboard with caution, since a dense training field and
a dispersed test make cross validation an unreliable guide for neighbour methods, a trap that has reshuffled private
standings in past competitions.

7. The open frontier

The strongest reported result is a per well shape estimator near five feet that uses only a well and its own typewell,
which sets the direction, and our oracle ladder places it strictly between the perfect line and the perfect quadratic.
Given the section resolved coherence measurement, we can now say which levers remain physically consistent with the
data. The first is calibration rather than selection: a predictor trained against an operator with the correct toe
spectrum learns when it cannot know, and a correctly hedged tail on the ambiguous fifth of wells is worth roughly the
distance from our score to the leaders without resolving a single alias — our earlier synthetics were three to five
times too coherent in the fine band at the toe, which trains exactly the opposite behaviour, confident commitment.
The second is synthetic scale at the level of the leading teams, since the transfer curve through the measured operator
was still descending when generation speed limited us. A third, joint inference across the hidden test set, we can now
report on directly rather than speculate about: the test wells do cluster (Section 4), the code competition kernel does
see them all at once, and the channel is nevertheless closed — the dip field decorrelates between pad mates faster than
a single well's own dip error, and the log-to-log match inherits the same seven-foot alias structure. Neither remaining
lever can recover the self similar core, which the coherence floor bounds, but both attack the per well shape and the
tail hedge, which is where the recoverable error lives.

8. Reproducibility

The metric is pooled root mean squared error over post heel one foot samples. To reproduce the operator of Section 2,
pair each training well's gamma ray with the typewell gamma ray interpolated at the known true depth, fit the gain and
offset, estimate the residual standard deviation and lag one autocorrelation, and compute the magnitude squared
coherence between the two curves (Welch, one foot sampling, segment length 256, half overlap), separately for the full
lateral, the heel rows, and the toe rows; the constants are gain near 0.79, offset near 17.8, residual standard
deviation near 11, residual autocorrelation near 0.83, raw signal standard deviation near 17, and correlations 0.755,
0.81, and 0.665 for full, heel, and toe respectively. The synthetic of Section 4 applies a low pass filter matched to
the coherence curve and an autoregressive red residual to the real typewell sampled along a drawn linear surface, with
operator parameters sampled per well from the measured spread; a faithful synthetic must be validated against the toe
spectrum, not a single scalar correlation, since a scalar target of 0.63 to 0.66 can be met by a generator whose fine
band is several times too coherent. The trackers, the stack, the measurement scripts, and the training and inference
code accompany this note.

9. Conclusion

The community has mapped the ROGII tail carefully: a bimodal datum from orbital cyclicity, largely recoverable by heel
calibration, with a small self similar core where the midpoint is the optimal hedge, and a per well shape that a strong
single model can estimate to about five feet. We add the acquisition side of that picture. By measuring the tool
response operator and its coherence spectrum directly from the data — and resolving it by well section — we show that
the instrument low pass filters away the gamma ray band that would resolve the self similar core, and that on the toe,
where every scored row lives, that band is all but gone. This explains the degenerate likelihood from the side of the
measurement and bounds the irreducible error. Our deployable classical system reaches 6.955 with a median at the level
of the leading teams, and our exhaustive study, uncertainty analysis, and reproducible measurements are offered in that
spirit. The broader lesson is that on gamma ray alone a fraction of the geosteering alias is not merely computationally
hard. It sits against an information limit set by the measurement tool itself.
