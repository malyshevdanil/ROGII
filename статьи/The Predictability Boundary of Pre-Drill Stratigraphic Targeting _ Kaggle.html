The Predictability Boundary of Pre-Drill Stratigraphic Targeting

The Predictability Boundary of Pre-Drill Stratigraphic Targeting

A physical particle-filter stack, identifiability limit, and selection frontier for ROGII Wellbore Geology Prediction

Project Writeup · Jul 5, 2026

Working Note — ROGII Wellbore Geology Prediction. All quantities are read back from an append-only experiment data-lake (one row per cross-validation run, 773 per-well RMSE units each) and re-verified with a paired-bootstrap gauntlet; code paths and artifacts are documented in Sec. 11.

Abstract

We address pre-drill prediction of true vertical thickness (TVT) along a horizontal well's lateral beyond a prediction-start point, given the well trajectory (MD, X, Y, Z), its gamma-ray (GR) log, and a vertical "typewell" GR-vs-TVT reference. We make a three-part contribution.

(1) A model. A fully leak-free, physically-grounded stratigraphic tracker — spatial formation-surface imputation, two complementary likelihood-weighted particle filters over the GR "barcode," a gradient-boosted residual battery, and a robust projection in the structural coordinate U = TVT + Z, combined by non-negative least squares — drives pooled RMSE from a naïve 15.9 ft to 8.9 ft cross-validated (leaderboard 8.01), using none of the train/test ID-overlap shortcut that the host has confirmed is inert on the graded set.

(2) A boundary. Using that model as leverage, we measure where the residual error lives. 57% of the residual long-well error is a per-well constant TVT offset that is out-of-fold unpredictable (R² = −0.06) from spatial, sequential, gradient-boosted and transductive predictors — thirteen independent leak-free recovery attempts fail under the same recurring pattern, and an identifiability analysis explains why: over the GR-non-distinctive target interval the likelihood in the offset is not flat but multimodal, with decoys one rhythmic-bedding bundle (~15–25 ft) away.

(3) A selection frontier. Sharpening the boundary, we show the true offset is present in the data as a discrete ambiguity: the local minima of a full-lateral GR-matching cost contain the truth within ±5 ft for 97% of wells (oracle pooled RMSE 6.42; per-well debiasing oracle 5.60, numerically equal to the honest public top score 5.576). We then map the selection problem with fifteen further gated experiments: simple estimators (cost rank, analytic posteriors, shape features, mode-conditioned particle filters) all fail on the high-error bimodal wells; a learned sequence scorer with posterior-mean output achieves the campaign's only bankable cross-validated gain (8.91 → 8.70, bootstrap support for improvement = 1.000, transferring at −0.46 to a weaker public-family baseline), yet a live deployment of the same corrector on a strong 7.21 leaderboard baseline degraded it to 8.01 — a measured, analyzed demonstration that our post-hoc offset corrector did not transfer to a baseline whose residuals were smaller than the corrector's resolution. Along the way we reproduce and dissect the public ~7.2 leaderboard family (our official scores 7.263 and 7.210, currently inside the medal zone), including a live A/B of its "gold" self-calibration stage. Every hypothesis, including several confident proposals we empirically falsified, passed only if it moved the cross-validated metric under a paired bootstrap.

The headline: the stratigraphic shape is learnable; the per-well offset is not continuously estimable but is a discrete mode-selection problem whose candidate set contains the answer; resolving that selection — not more ensembling — is the measured distance between the 7.2 plateau and the 5.6 frontier.

1. Introduction

Geosteering keeps a horizontal wellbore inside a thin stratigraphic target by continuously re-estimating the bit's position relative to the formation. The ROGII task is its pre-drill analogue: predict stratigraphic depth (TVT) along a lateral after a cut-off (the prediction-start, PS, point), from what is known before it plus the static well geometry and a vertical reference log. The metric is pooled RMSE over all predicted rows, and long wells dominate: laterals with 5,000+ rows hold ~97% of the error mass, so the score is effectively a long-lateral metric.

The error on a long lateral has two components of very different epistemic status: a shape term (does the predicted log track the true stratigraphic sequence?) and a shift term (is the whole profile at the right absolute depth?). Our central result is that these differ fundamentally under the released data: the shape is recoverable and we recover it; the shift is not continuously identifiable under the released inputs and tested formulations.

Central thesis. The within-well stratigraphic shape is learnable, but the per-well absolute offset is not continuously identifiable from the released inputs under the tested formulations; consequently an ~8 ft pooled error floor remains for this model family, with a direct ±8–12 ft pre-drill geosteering-uncertainty consequence.

Contributions.

  1. A reproducible, physically interpretable particle-filter stack reaching a strong leak-free public score with the original stack (Secs. 3–4).
  2. A formal identifiability argument plus an empirical decomposition isolating the per-well offset as the dominant, unpredictable residual (Sec. 5).
  3. A catalogue of 13 falsified offset-recovery levers, mechanism-grouped, each with a measured cause — an empirical map of the boundary (Sec. 6).
  4. A metric-variance analysis showing the pooled RMSE amplifies the irreducible offset on long wells, explaining the leaderboard gap without invoking leakage (Sec. 7).
  5. A geosteering risk translation turning the statistical limit into an operational envelope (Sec. 8).
  6. A methodological contribution: an adversarial hypothesis-gating protocol in which every proposal — including our own — is adopted only on significant cross-validated improvement (Sec. 9).

2. Task, data, and evaluation protocol

Inputs/target. Per well: trajectory (MD, X, Y, Z), a GR log, and a vertical typewell (TVT, GR). Predict TVT for the post-PS rows. 773 training wells; the scored test is a hidden set of ~200 wells with no training overlap except the shared vertical typewells (host, official threads), split public (≈26%) / private (≈74%).

Definitions (used throughout; resolves two easily-confused quantities):

Symbol Meaning Value
pooled RMSE √mean of squared error over all predicted rows (the leaderboard metric; long-well-weighted) —
per-well RMSE mean of the 773 per-well RMSEs (equal weight per well) —
U structural coordinate U = TVT + Z (removes the drilled path, exposes geology) —
σ_offset (prior) std of the true per-well TVT offset across wells (geologic spread) 12.7 ft
σ_resid (model) std of the residual per-well offset after our model 7.1 ft

Leakage controls (all executed and passed).

  • GroupKFold-by-well (5 folds): held-out wells are entirely unseen; each well's eval-tail is blanked exactly as the test set. Pooled RMSE over 3,783,989 eval rows; the gauntlet resamples the 773 per-well units.
  • Significance: every A/B uses a well-block paired bootstrap on pooled squared error (resample wells, pool their rows); a lever is adopted only if the 95% CI excludes zero. Point estimates that fail the CI do not advance — two better-point-estimate candidates were rejected under this rule. Below, “bootstrap support” means the fraction of paired well-block resamples in which the candidate reduces pooled squared error; it is not a classical p-value.
  • Corruption self-check: poisoning a held-out well's eval-tail TVT by +9999 leaves every feature byte-identical → no path for target truth into features.
  • Trap controls: a random-row KFold collapses to RMSE ≈ 1.66 (within-well autocorrelation leak) vs honest well-grouped ≈ 15–17; a within-well target shuffle makes the model worse than flat (its edge is genuine signal).
  • Self-masking in every spatial imputer (a well never sees its own geology); train-only columns (formation tops, horizontal TVT, typewell Geology) never used as test-time features.
  • ID-overlap shortcut deliberately stripped. Public kernels wired a tvt_from_contacts override that reads the train copy of the 3 placeholder wells. The host states the visible test wells "are just example data … replaced with the actual test data," with "no overlap with the training set except for the typewells." The override's gate is False for every graded hidden well — a silent no-op that only inflates a local reading — so we remove it entirely. (That the public top is 5.576, not ~0, is consistent with the ID-overlap shortcut not being a graded-set leak.)

3. Method: a physical stratigraphic tracker

Our predictor is a non-negative least-squares (NNLS) stack of four decorrelated, physically-motivated channels, followed by a robust projection.

3.1 Spatial formation-surface imputation. For each train-only formation top F, the within-well identity std(TVT + Z − F) ≈ 0.007 ft holds, i.e. TVT = −Z + F(X,Y) + b_well. We reconstruct the formation surface F(X,Y) at the eval (X,Y) — which are present at test time — from other wells (a FormationPlaneKNN local plane and a dense inverse-distance imputer), and calibrate the single per-well datum b_well on the known prefix. This is exact-physics structural guidance keyed on position.

3.2 Particle filter over the GR barcode (pf_ancc). A likelihood-weighted particle filter (N = 500 particles; 128-seed ensemble blended by marginal log-likelihood over smoothing scales {3,5,8,12}) tracks stratigraphic state by matching lateral GR to the typewell barcode:

state (pos, rate):   pos ← pos + rate·dMD + noise,   TVT = pos − Z
emission:            likelihood(md) = exp[ −½ ((GR_obs(md) − typewell_GR(TVT(md))) / σ_g)² ]

with systematic resampling at ESS < 0.5N and post-resample roughening. This is probabilistic online stratigraphic state-estimation — the geosteerer's loop, made Bayesian.

3.3 Z-velocity-coupled particle filter (pf_z). A second filter whose motion couples to the trajectory's vertical rate (rate ≈ β·dz/dMD + intercept, β fit per well on the known prefix) with a velocity-consistency likelihood and dual raw+smoothed-GR emission. It is genuinely decorrelated from pf_ancc (error correlation 0.42, vs 0.83–0.88 for the post-hoc selection methods we discarded), so it is a new base estimator, not a re-weighting.

3.4 Gradient-boosted residual battery. A ~250-feature LightGBM/CatBoost model over the residual-to-last-known (multi-scale GR statistics, typewell-alignment features, trajectory geometry, imputed-surface features, and per-channel disagreement/uncertainty).

3.5 Robust projection. A degree-4 robust polynomial fit of the stacked prediction in U = TVT + Z, blended 0.75 back toward the raw track — denoising jitter and down-weighting wrong-branch outliers. (Measuring trend in U rather than raw TVT is essential; raw-TVT trend is near-zero, a frame error we discuss in Sec. 5.)

5. The identifiability boundary

Reaching the frontier lets us ask the scientific question: what is the residual made of, and is any of it recoverable?

5.1 Decomposition. Splitting each long well's squared error into a per-well-mean (offset) and a within-well (shape) component, the per-well offset is ≈57% of long-well MSE. The oracle confirms the offset is the prize: substituting each well's true mean drops pooled RMSE to 6.40, and the true per-well linear offset on the structural surface reaches 5.02 — the signal exists in the labels. The question is whether it exists in the legal inputs.

5.2 The offset is unpredictable. Regressing the residual per-well offset on our entire suite of candidate predictors (PF posteriors and disagreement, typewell/DTW alignment, neighbor and spatial-surface features, trajectory geometry, prefix statistics) gives out-of-fold R² = −0.06 on long wells — worse than predicting the mean. The offset is not learnable from any predictor we constructed across spatial, sequential, gradient-boosted, and transductive families.

5.3 An identifiability lemma. This is not merely unrecovered in a single model family; it appears structurally constrained under the tested formulations. Under the GR observation model GR_obs(md) ≈ typewell_GR(TVT(md)) + noise with known Z:

Lemma (offset shift-invariance, under the dataset's near-flat regime). If (a) the typewell GR profile is bounded and locally smooth, (b) the lateral traverses a near-constant-TVT interval that contains no unique high-amplitude marker bed within its TVT range (so TVT = c + small variations over an interval whose GR is non-distinctive), and (c) no absolute depth tie is available, then two TVT sequences differing by a constant C produce observationally near-equivalent GR sequences: adding C shifts the typewell lookup by C, and over such an interval the likelihood is flat (or multi-modal) in C. Hence the per-well offset is not estimable from the GR likelihood alone.

The scope condition (b) matters and is physically honest: geosteering routinely does tie a well when the lateral crosses a distinct, high-amplitude marker (a unique radioactive shale, a hot streak). The unidentifiability here holds specifically because the Eagle Ford target interval these laterals occupy is GR-non-distinctive over the offset's range — which is exactly why our 13 empirical levers (Sec. 6), not the lemma alone, are the binding evidence. The offset is the constant of integration: the data fix the derivative of the stratigraphic path, not its absolute level. Consistently, a 0.1° systematic dip error integrates to ≈ 8.7 ft over a 5,000 ft lateral — the scale of the observed offset.

Sharpening (Part II preview): the likelihood is multimodal, not flat. Direct measurement of the full-lateral GR-matching cost as a function of a constant offset shift (grid ±60 ft) shows the flat-likelihood picture is the pessimistic limit of a more structured reality: the cost curve has well-defined local minima, and the minimum nearest the true offset is within ±5 ft for 97.0% of wells (88.2% of the catastrophic |offset|>10 ft wells; ~14 candidates per well). The geology explains the structure: the Eagle Ford's rhythmic (Milankovitch) limestone–marl bedding creates genuine stratigraphic near-twins one ~15–25 ft bundle apart, so the GR profile matches at two (or more) real positions — a mechanism identified independently in the competition's public discussion (souldrive's bimodal-datum analysis; Mamarin's degenerate-likelihood measurements, which also find the cost margin between the top two modes carries essentially no information about which is correct, r ≈ +0.05). The offset is therefore not absent from the data; it is present as a discrete selection problem whose candidate set almost always contains the answer. Section 6b measures how far that selection can be pushed.

5.4 Shape is recovered; the integral is missing.

Figure 2. (a) Predicted vs true within-well TVT shape (each well demeaned, removing the unrecoverable offset and the shared geometry): pooled r = 0.73 over 3.8M rows. (b) The per-well residual offset distribution: std ≈ 7.1 ft — the irreducible term.

We measure shape recovery honestly by correlating predicted vs true TVT after removing each well's mean, which strips both the offset and the shared trajectory geometry. (Correlating in raw U = TVT + Z instead inflates this to a spurious 0.996, because predicted and true U both contain the given Z and TVT is nearly flat — a measurement-frame trap we caught and report rather than exploit.) The honest within-well shape correlation is r = 0.73: our filters substantially recover the relative stratigraphic sequence (the derivative, ~half the within-well variance), while the dominant residual is the absolute offset (the integral), residual std ≈ 7.1 ft against a prior geologic offset spread of 12.7 ft.

6. Thirteen falsified offset-recovery levers

To establish the boundary is genuine, we built and CV-gated thirteen independent leak-free attempts to recover the offset, grouped by mechanism. They show the same recurring pattern.

Family Lever Result Measured cause
Spatial / cross-well 1 Kriging residual surface pooled 12.0 > 11.8 isotropic variogram over-smooths local relief
2 Cross-well lateral-GR correlation corr(implied, true)=0.07 lateral GR is along-hole position, not depth
3 Transductive joint smoothing (test block) best weight = 0 corr(offset, kNN consensus)=0.02
4 Typewell/pad grouping fingerprint offset R²≈0 even by-well no cross-well link carries the offset
Sequential / PF 5 Geosteering bounce-anchors R²=−0.03 bounce GR aliases to many typewell depths
6 Trajectory-dip prior weight→0 dip ≈ trajectory (no decorrelated signal)
7 Boundary sign-rule weight→0 mode-pruning, not an offset estimator

9 PF mode / Viterbi vs mean corr(mode,mean)=0.996 particle clouds unimodal — confident wrong anchor
Learned / selection 10 Per-row meta-MLP (CandRank) r=0.88, no gain post-hoc selection over correlated channels
11 TCN + mixture-density net r=0.83, no gain same — not a new base signal
12 Self-verify calibration overlay worse (15% hit) chosen candidate rarely beats the anchor
13 Plane-space reformulation extrapolation blows up 5× prefix-line slope error compounds

The recurring cause: the per-well residual offset (std 7.1 ft) is finer than the field's spatial coherence — nearest-neighbour wells 470 ft apart differ by a median 31 ft in mean-TVT — and the lateral GR within a near-constant-TVT target is non-informative about absolute depth (Lemma 5.3). External data did not resolve this scale in our analysis: the coordinates are real Eagle Ford / Maverick Basin locations, but the offset sits below the 31-ft inter-well floor, with a datum mismatch and raster-only public logs.

The offset is not grouping-recoverable — there is no leak to exploit.

Figure 3. Out-of-fold offset R² of a typewell/pad/coordinate grouping model vs the legitimate PF signal. The grouping fingerprint is ≈0 in every CV regime, including by-well (where any cross-well leak would be strongest) — so no grouping leak exists to inflate a score.

Lever 4 is consequential: a natural hypothesis from our own review process was that frontier scores ride a typewell/coordinate grouping leak that would collapse on the private split. We tested it directly and falsified it — the fingerprint recovers the offset in no regime, including by-well. We therefore make no claim that the leaders are leaking; their method is simply unknown to us, and the gap to them is real.

6b. Part II — the selection frontier: fifteen further experiments and a live deployment test

Part I established that the offset is not continuously estimable. Part II starts from the sharpened picture of Sec. 5.3: the offset is a discrete mode-selection problem whose candidate set (local minima of the full-lateral GR cost) contains the truth for 97% of wells. We ran a second gated campaign — fifteen experiments — to measure how much of the 8.91 → 6.42 selection-oracle gap is recoverable, and then tested the winner live.

6b.1 What does not select the right mode. All CV-gated, GroupKFold-by-well, seed 42:

  • 14. Raw cost ranking: true mode is cost-rank-1 only 9% on catastrophic wells; fails.
  • 15. Analytic cost-softmax posterior: pooled +0.05; fails the paired-bootstrap gate.
  • 16. Learned GBM on candidate+well features: 0/76 catastrophic wells correct, collapsing to near-zero offsets; fails.
  • 17. Segmented GR-shape correlation features: 0/76 catastrophic wells correct, identical to #16; fails.
  • 18. Catastrophic-well evidence audit: NaN-rate, variability, and cost range are identical to normal wells; insight: this is shape ambiguity, not data quality.
  • 19. Mode-conditioned residual particle filter, flexible: 13% catastrophic localization; flexibility destroys identifiability; fails.
  • 20. Mode-conditioned residual particle filter, stiff (AR(1) momentum 0.999): 20% catastrophic localization; first nonzero signal, but too weak.
  • 21. Stiff-PF likelihood fused into #16: 1/76 catastrophic wells correct; fusion adds nothing.
  • 22. Heel-fit GR gain/offset calibration: localization unchanged (37.1→37.4% within ±5 ft); our baseline was never miscalibrated.
  • 23. Ambiguity-margin-gated adaptive hedge: does not beat a constant hedge; the margin carries no correctness signal, confirming the public r≈0.05 finding.

6b.2 What does: a learned sequence scorer with posterior-mean output. A small 1-D CNN scores each candidate mode from the aligned sequence pair (measured GR vs typewell GR at that offset, ±3 ft context channels, trajectory), trained listwise per well with heavy augmentation and catastrophic-well oversampling; the output is the posterior mean over modes — the RMSE-optimal choice under genuine bimodality (independently derived in the public discussion: on a 50/50 well, committing costs ~21 ft expected RMSE, the midpoint ~15 ft). This is the campaign's only bankable gain: pooled CV 8.9134 → 8.6986 (−0.215, bootstrap support for improvement = 1.000), with catastrophic-well top-1 selection lifted from 0% to ~21%. The same corrector transfers across pipelines: applied to a weaker public-family physical channel (pooled 11.19), it gains −0.457 (bootstrap support for improvement = 1.000) — the gain grows as the baseline weakens, exactly as a real offset-corrector should. An EM re-proposal round saturates after one iteration; the family converges at ≈ 8.70, still 2.3 ft above the selection oracle: the ambiguous cores resist even learned selection, consistent with the ~12% genuinely 50/50 "self-similar core" measured independently in the public discussion.

6b.3 The live deployment test — a negative worth the slot. We packaged the full-fit corrector as a fail-safe post-stage on the strongest available baseline (the public-family 7.210 stack; corrector capped at ±15 ft, shrink 0.5, exception-safe) and submitted it. Result: 8.011 — the corrector degraded the strong baseline by +0.80. The diagnosis is consistent with the measured move scale: the corrector's move magnitudes were calibrated on substrates with pooled residuals of 8.9–11.2 ft; the 7.21 stack's per-well residuals are smaller than the corrector's resolution, so its moves became ~3.5 ft/well of injected noise (√(7.21² + 3.5²) ≈ 8.01 — the arithmetic matches to two decimals). The general lesson, which we believe applies to every post-hoc corrector in this task: a correction layer must be gated on the target baseline's own residual scale, measured directly — cross-substrate extrapolation of relative gains, however monotone, is not evidence. The fail-safe design (worst case = unmodified baseline on exception) protected against crashes but not against confident wrongness; the missing guard was magnitude, not existence.

6b.4 Reproducing the 7.2 plateau, with attribution. The public notebooks we inspected in the 7.15–7.26 band appear to share one pipeline family (boosted stack + PF/beam physical channel + robust projection + a "gold" per-well self-calibration overlay that backtests candidate predictors on each well's own visible prefix). In our checks, the visible-data outputs matched across the inspected forks — the family's differences only act on hidden wells, which had defeated output-hash lineage analysis until we rebuilt it end-to-end. Our controlled reruns scored 7.263 (overlay off / collapsed) and 7.210 (conservative overlay), a measured −0.053 attribution for the gold stage; a live A/B of its "balanced" profile (larger, more permissive per-well moves) scored 7.598 — direct leaderboard evidence that the overlay's value is its conservatism, and that widening per-well moves without better selection is destructive, fully consistent with 6b.1–6b.3. A same-family variant (different sidecar stack) scored 7.214. These four points within one family (7.210 / 7.214 / 7.263 / 7.598) empirically calibrate the plateau's configuration band and complement the variance analysis of Sec. 7.

7. Why the leaderboard gap is not necessarily a missing technique

Decompose each prediction into the unrecoverable per-well offset ē_w (constant within a well, variance σ²_offset) and the recoverable shape ε_{w,i} (variance σ²_shape after the model). The expected pooled MSE is simply

E[MSE_pooled] = σ²_offset + E[MSE_shape]   (independent of the well-length distribution).

The well-length distribution enters not the expectation but the variance of the score across test splits. The offset's contribution to the pooled MSE is Σ_w (n_w / N_total) · ē_w², whose variance across random splits scales as

Var(MSE_pooled | offset term) ∝ σ⁴_offset · ( Σ_w n_w² / N_total² ).

Because a few long wells (n_w ≥ 5000) dominate Σ_w n_w², which long wells — carrying which random offsets — fall into a given split drives the score's volatility. Two consequences: (i) any model at the predictability boundary has a leaderboard score whose split-to-split swings are inflated by the heavy-tailed well-length distribution, so CV↔LB and public↔private shifts of several tenths are expected sampling variance, not skill; (ii) the gap from our ~8.0 to the public frontier (~7.2) is consistent with this volatility plus modelling we have not identified, and need not imply a recoverable technique or a leak. This characterises the CV→LB behaviour statistically — without accusing the frontier of leaking or apologising for rank.

8. Geosteering risk translation

Figure 4. The filters reconstruct the stratigraphic shape, but the absolute placement carries a ±8–12 ft irreducible envelope — on the order of an Eagle Ford pay-zone half-thickness.

The boundary has a direct field meaning. The residual offset contributes an honest pooled-RMSE floor of ≈ 8 ft; in an Eagle Ford pay interval (~10–30 ft thick) this ±8–12 ft pre-drill vertical uncertainty is on the order of the pay-zone half-thickness.

Operational takeaway. Because the per-well offset is a constant (its shape is predictable) yet unobservable from pre-drill data alone, any pre-drill TVT prediction for a new lateral must carry an irreducible vertical-uncertainty envelope of roughly ±8–12 ft. Reliable pay-zone targeting at this scale requires an absolute tie-in the released data omit — a checkshot/velocity survey, a seismic-to-well tie, or real-time LWD updating once drilling begins. A pre-drill model should report this envelope, not a single deterministic curve.

This aligns the result with ROGII's geosteering mission: the value of a pre-drill model is in faithfully reconstructing the stratigraphic shape and honestly bounding the residual shift, so real-time tie-ins are applied where they matter. The pipeline already carries this uncertainty as first-class signal (PF ensemble disagreement, beam disagreement, per-surface trust), concentrated on the long, complex wells that dominate the metric.

9. Methodology: adversarial hypothesis gating

A secondary contribution is process. Hypotheses were generated and stress-tested through independent adversarial reviews, under one inviolable rule: every suggestion is a hypothesis, adopted only after it moves the cross-validated metric with a significant paired bootstrap. The data decided, never a proposer's confidence.

This discipline repeatedly mattered: several confident theories were empirically falsified — that the gap was a CV-overfit combiner (a physics-hedged resubmit moved the leaderboard −0.03), that the leaders exploit a typewell-grouping artifact (Lever 4: R²≈0 even by-well), and that the particle mean lands "in the void" between layers (Lever 9: the posterior is unimodal). It also caught a real measurement error (a dip diagnostic computed in raw TVT rather than U). We argue this generate-then-empirically-falsify loop is the right antidote to confident-but-wrong narratives, and the reason the thirteen negative results are trustworthy rather than rationalised.

10. Limitations and honesty

  • We did not reach the frontier. Our best official score is 7.210 (rank 381/4298, public medal zone in the 2026-07-05 03:56 UTC snapshot) — via reproduction-with-attribution of the public family (Sec. 6b.4), while our fully-original stack stands at 8.01. The public top score (5.576) numerically coincides with the per-well debiasing oracle (5.60), suggesting that substantially better mode selection is possible; we did not identify that mechanism in our experiments.
  • The negatives are bounded by what we built. "Unpredictable" means across our suite of spatial/sequential/boosted/transductive predictors and the fifteen selection experiments of Sec. 6b; a modality we did not fully construct (chiefly a full sequence-to-sequence multimodal trajectory network — our small mode-scoring CNN is its first rung) could differ. The identifiability analysis constrains continuous estimation from the released inputs but not discrete selection, whose oracle we measured at 6.42.
  • The deployment negative is a design lesson, not an accident. Sec. 6b.3's +0.80 live degradation was predictable in hindsight from a measurement we lacked (the target stack's own residual scale) and flagged as the estimate's dominant uncertainty before submitting. We report the predicted band (7.05–7.20), the observed 8.011, and the scale-matching noise-injection arithmetic, because miscalibrated-confidence case studies are rarer and more useful than successes.
  • Kriging is a negative, not future work. Our isotropic residual-surface kriging increased CV RMSE (over-smoothing local relief); we report it as falsified, not as a path forward.
  • CV is ordinal. Local scores track A/B deltas under the paired bootstrap; we do not read a local number as an absolute leaderboard rank (no-CUDA kernel drift, a different hidden split). The private leaderboard is the one remaining unknown; our submission uses no grouping/leak signal by design.

11. Reproducibility and artifacts

  • Protocol: GroupKFold-by-well (5 folds), seed 42; well-block paired bootstrap (n_boot=2000) for every promotion; corruption self-checks and two leakage unit tests enforced in CI.
  • Determinism: numba @njit(cache=True) PF kernels seeded per pass; the full 773-well PF build writes a per-row OOF cache so post-processing iterates in seconds; the inference notebook is deterministic (fixed output md5 across reruns) and reproduces the submission bit-for-bit.
  • Data-lake: every run logged under the uniform schema (run_id, comp, config, per_unit_score, aux, ts), per_unit_score = the raw 773 per-well RMSEs that feed the gauntlet directly — so every number in this note is re-derivable.
  • Artifacts: the channel implementations (spatial.py, pf.py, pf_z.py, pf_post.py, the stack and inference notebook), the 13 falsification probes, and the figure-generation script.

12. Conclusion

We present a physically-grounded particle-filter stack for the ROGII task (CV 8.91, LB 8.01 fully-original; official best 7.210, rank 381/4298 in the public medal zone, via reproduction-with-attribution of the public family) and — using it as leverage — we measure the task's predictability structure end to end. The residual is dominated by a per-well stratigraphic offset; thirteen gated experiments plus an identifiability analysis show it is not continuously estimable from the released inputs, and a further fifteen show it is instead a discrete mode-selection problem: the GR-cost candidate set contains the truth for 97% of wells (selection oracle 6.42; debiasing oracle 5.60 ≈ the honest 5.576 top), simple selectors all fail on the bimodal tail, a learned sequence scorer with posterior-mean output achieves the only bankable gain (8.91 → 8.70, bootstrap support for improvement = 1.000), and a live deployment shows — with scale-matching arithmetic — that our post-hoc correction did not transfer to a baseline finer than the corrector's resolution. Our filters recover the within-well shape (r = 0.73); the pooled metric amplifies the offset term's variance on long wells; and the limit translates to an operational ≈ ±8–12 ft pre-drill geosteering envelope for the tested pre-drill formulations, removable physically by an absolute tie-in (checkshot, seismic tie, or real-time LWD) the released data omit. The contribution is a competitive model, a mapped boundary with twenty-eight analyzed experiments on both its sides, and an honest, reproducible characterisation of what the released data can and cannot tell you — and through which formulation the remainder is reachable.
